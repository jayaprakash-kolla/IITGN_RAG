W H AT ’ S G O I N G O N ?
1. A fraction is a number that represents a part of a whole.
It is written as a numerator displayed above a line (or
before a slash), and a denominator, displayed below (or
after) that line.
2. For example 2/3 (above and below) or 2/3 (before and

3. The numerator represents a number of equal parts, and

Story of Gauss
One day, a teacher wrote numbers from 1 to 100 and
asked his students to calculate the sum of all the numbers.
All the students started adding the numbers. Initially, the
process was fast like an express train, but when they
reached the middle numbers, it became a passenger train.
One boy was sitting at the back, starting at the board and
doing nothing. The teacher got angry, and asked him why
isn’t is adding the numbers.The students remained quiet
for some time, and then suddenly, said 5050.
He recognized that the first and last number added
together give 101. The second and second last added
together also give 101 and so on. And there are 50 such
pairs with sum equal to 101. Therefore, the total sum is
equal to 50 × 101 = 5050
This child, later became the most famous mathematician,
Carl Friedrich Gauss. This story depicts that math is
not just about formulas, but interesting patterns!

SUM OF GP (SUM = 1/3)
LEARNINGS

Area of a Square

Can you add a series that has infinite number of terms?
Would the answer still be a finite number? Let's find out in
this activity.

W H AT T O D O ?
1. Take out the pieces marked as 1/4, 1/16, 1/64,...from the
square and the triangle
2. Observe that these pieces make 1/3rd of the whole square
and triangle.

112

NOTES

O B S E RVAT I O N
1. The side of the big square is taken as 1 unit. Therefore
the area of the square is 1 square units.
2. If you divide the square in four equal parts, the area of
that piece would be 1/4.
3. If you further divide that part in four equal parts, the area
would be 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16 and so on. So the area of all the
three pieces is equal to 1/4 + 1/16 + 1/64 + 1/256 + ......
4. The square is divided in three equal parts by these pieces.
So the area of one piece is equal to 1/3rd of the area of
whole square = 1/3
5. Therefore, 1/4 + 1/16 + 1/64 + 1/256 + ....... = 1/3

W H AT ’ S G O I N G O N ?
The sequence 1/4, 1/16, 1/64,..... is an example of Geometric
series. It's a series where ratio of two consecutive terms is
same.
In this case, the ratio is 1/4 for any two consecutive terms.
You can have another sequence where the difference is some
other number. For example, in 1, 4, 16, 64,...(and so on), the
difference between consecutive terms is 4. If this ratio is
more than 1, the sum of infinite terms won't be a finite
number.
