New Initiative: Institute Archives
» The Institute Archives at IIT Gandhinagar (IITGN) is established to preserve the institutional memory

and document the vibrant journey of the Institute since its inception. Over the past 16 years, IITGN
has grown rapidly, making significant contributions in the areas of academics, research, innovation,
student life, planning and development, and administration. These years have witnessed major
milestones and memorable events that form an important part of the Institute’s heritage.

» As IITGN continues to evolve, it generates a wide variety of information, ranging from documents,

photographs, reports, publications, media files to personal collections and institutional records.
Much of this valuable material is scattered and unorganized. Sensing the importance of preserving
this legacy, the Institute Archives was initiated with a centralized and structured effort to collect,
curate, preserve, and make this diverse range of historical material, accessible to the community at
large.

ACCESS POLICY FOR E-RESOURCES
Who can access e-resources?
• Faculty, students, staff & others directly associated with IITGN

How to access?
• IP based access on Campus & Some require login ID & Password
• Remote access (off campus access available)

Copyright / License Agreement
• Copyright regulations apply same way as printed books & journals
• Electronic journals & databases access is based on license agreements with publishers / vendors.
• https://library.iitgn.ac.in/librarypolicy.php?type=eresources

What's not permitted?
• Systematic downloading, sharing with outside users, using any software to download entire database,
creating your collection of entire, using it for commercial purpose

BE AWARE OF PREDATORY PUBLISHING?...
Word ‘Predatory’ was coined by by Jeffry Beall in 2010

…predatory publishers…publish counterfeit journals to exploit
the open-access model in which the author pays. These
predatory publishers are dishonest and lack transparency. They
aim to dupe researchers, especially those inexperienced in
scholarly communication.
Beall, J. (2012) “Predatory publishers are corrupting open access”, Nature, vol. 489 iss. 7415.
