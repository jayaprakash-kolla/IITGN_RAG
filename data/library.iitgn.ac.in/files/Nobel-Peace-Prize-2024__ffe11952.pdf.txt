The Nihon Hidankyo or Hibakusha Movement for a World Free of Nuclear Weapons: A Critical
Appreciation
By Tanka Subba, Visiting Professor, HSS
In 1945, two American atom bombs killed an estimated 140,000 inhabitants of Hiroshima and
70,000 inhabitants of Nagasaki. A comparable number died of burn and radiation injuries in the
ensuing months and years. The two Japanese cities were completely burnt and devastated. Many of
the nearly 650,000 survivors suffered from psychological trauma and physical suffering for life.
Nihon Hidankyo, the grassroots movement of atomic and hydrogen bomb survivors’ organizations
from Hiroshima and Nagasaki, established in 1956 at Nagasaki, received the Nobel Peace Prize for
2024 for its untiring efforts to achieve a world free of nuclear weapons and for demonstrating,
through witness testimonies, that nuclear weapons must never be used again. Yet, the fact remains
that today there are more than 12 thousand nuclear weapons with far greater destructive power
than the two bombs used by America against Japan. They can kill millions of us in an instant,
disable us forever, and damage our environment by contaminating it with radioactive substance. A
nuclear war can destroy an entire city today. And that possibility cannot be ruled out when some
of the countries having nuclear bombs today are ruled by despots or are suffering from political
instability, either of which is not a good situation for a world free from nuclear warfare. Such
countries can opt for a nuclear war if their rulers find it politically expedient to do so or if it is
important for their own survival.
It is a fact that most houses in the two cities were crushed by the shock waves of the atom bombs
killing most inmates. Some were directly burned by heat rays, and others had the gamma rays
penetrating their bodies and destroying them from inside, killing or maiming them for life. Almost
everyone who lived in the two cities suffered from injuries and exposure to radiation. Yet, they
were silenced, neglected and stigmatized, first by the occupying American forces for seven years
and then on by their own Japanese government. The Hibakushas (survivors) had to silently spend
their lives in isolation, combating illness and hardship in their lives, while also enduring prejudice
and discrimination.
I argue in this lecture that the prospect of a nuclear weapon-free world looks grim, as none of the
nine countries that possess nuclear weapons, as of 2024 – the United States, Russia, China,
France, the United Kingdom, India, Pakistan, Israel and North Korea – appear interested in
nuclear disarmament and nuclear arms control at present. On the contrary, they are modernizing
and piling up their nuclear arsenals because of a rather toothless United Nations as our guardian.
There are palpable indications to show that the nuclear power countries are not taking any
initiative to downgrade their nuclear arsenal. The nuclear power countries like Pakistan, Israel,
North Korea, and our own country India have not even ratified the Treaty on the Prohibition of
Nuclear Weapons (TPNW) as yet.
