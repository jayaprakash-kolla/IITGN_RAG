Scholarly Publishing Models
• Web has revolutionized the way how scholarly work is disseminated
• Platform for publishing, distributing & accessing scholarly content
oScholarly publishing models have/still are undergoing a major change
◦ Traditional model
◦ Open Access (2002) OA model: Literature in digital, online, free of charge and free of
most copyright and licensing restrictions
◦ Gold – Journals- depend on revenue through Article Processing Charge
◦ Hybrid- Subscription based journal where selected articles published on paying APC
◦ Green – E-Print/Preprint Archives, Institutional Repositories
◦ Diamond/Platinum- Third party funded (organization, nationally, community driven)
◦ Bronze- Institutions, Associations, non-profit making- no right re-use (no open
license)

What is predatory publishing?...
Word ‘Predatory’ was coined by Jeffry Beall in 2010
Means preying naturally on others, seeking to exploit others
“…predatory publishers…publish counterfeit journals to exploit the open-access
model in which the author pays. These predatory publishers are dishonest and
lack transparency. They aim to dupe researchers, especially those inexperienced
in scholarly communication.”
Beall, J. (2012) “Predatory publishers are corrupting open access”, Nature, vol.
489 iss. 7415.

What is predatory publishing?...
"Predatory journals and publishers are entities that prioritize self-interest at the
expense of scholarship and are characterized by false or misleading information,
deviation from best editorial and publication practices, a lack of transparency,
and/or the use of aggressive and indiscriminate solicitation practices."
◦ Grudniewicz, A. Science (576)7786. 2019 210-212.

In nutshell, “Predatory journals are deceptive and often fake, giving appearance
of legitimate peer-reviewed journals and impact academic stake holders by
exploiting the OA model while using misleading tactics to solicit article
submissions” Simon Linacre The Predator Effect, 2022

Predatory publishers - common characteristics
• Ultimate goal is to make money - not to publish scholarly research
• Use deception to appear legitimate
• Make false claims about services offered (peer review)
• Unethical business practices
• Exploit the need for academics to publish
• No concern for quality of work published
• Do not follow accepted scholarly publishing best practices

Common Features

Predatory Journal

1

Peer-review process

•

Claims to have a peer-review process but does not provide this or uses duplicated templated reports

2

Editorial service

•
•
•

Offers a timeline that would be unrealistic for publication with a robust peer-review process eg <24 hours
Publishes articles with spelling/grammar mistakes
No plagiarism screening or copyediting

3

Transparency

•
•

Publishes articles with an APC even if low quality or not within the scope of the journal.
Lacks transparency about the APC, with some not advertising this upfront, only stating it after the article is accepted

4

Business practices

•

Aggressively targets authors via solicitation emails that are persuasive in language style and may contain grammar/spelling
mistakes

5

Publishing practices

•

•
•

Requires authors to sign away copyright at the submission stage, preventing them from withdrawing and preventing
publishing elsewhere
Publishes articles before authors have signed publishing agreements
Publishes articles on temporary websites which may be removed and do not inform the authors

6

Website

•

States offices in a country which does not align with time stamps of solicitation emails or phone number country codes

7

Indexing and metrics

•
•

Claims to have an impact factor or citation metrics that cannot be verified
Mimics names of well-known, legitimate journals

Spotting Predatory Journals…
➢Journal website-web pages filled with bogus journals
➢Published articles- content, quality, details, format, references etc
➢Editorial Board & affiliation with inaccurate information
➢Unsolicited email invitations
➢Soliciting authors to be editors, reviewers, special issue editors
➢Rapid publication with peer review done
➢Unclear fees- too much variation

Spotting Predatory Journals…
▪Broad journal scope, misleading, mimicking existing established titles
▪Spelling & grammatical errors
▪Distorted or unauthorized images
▪Homepage targets authors
▪Fake metrics- impact factor, h-index…
▪Promotes questionable index sources –whether indexed WoS, Scopus…
▪Poor description of manuscript handling process

Spotting Predatory Journals…
▪Email manuscript submission
▪No retraction policy

▪Digital preservation information lacking
▪Low article processing charges
▪Generic contact email address (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.)

▪Copyright information lacking
▪Engaging in questionable business practices
▪Many other misleading activities

Simple rules for avoiding predatory publishing scams
• Be suspicious
• Assess the journal content
• Check publication fees
• Examine journals peer review standards
• Recognise the gatekeepers (editorial board/committee)
• Discover where the journal is indexed
• Verify claims to metrics
• Identify the publisher
• Go beyond lists
• Do not over react
• Finally, Ask a Librarian
(https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009377)

Predatory Conference
• Many predatory publishers are organizing fake conferences

• At glance, these conferences can seem to be legitimate and scientific based
events
• These conferences are organized by revenue generating companies who not
only exploit presenters & attendees, but also increase profits by collecting
registration fees
• These fake conferences do not provide adequate scholarly presentation
sessions
• Many academics arrive at these conferences only to discover that there are very
few attendees, a limited number of actual presentations, or that multiple
conferences covering a wide range of topics are combined into a single
conference

How to spot predatory conferences
▪ Conference Organizers and Sponsorship
▪ Aim & Scope
▪ Agenda & Editorial Committee
▪ Conference Location
▪ Poorly Organized
▪ Website
▪ Invitation
▪ ​Reputation
▪ Peer Review Process
▪ Conference Fees

Why Predatory Publishing is Harmful
➢Lack of peer-review
➢​Work could disappear
➢Work will be difficult to find- not indexed
➢Harmful to reputation
➢Peer group will not recognise

How is the situation being handled?
• Explicit warning from publishers, institutions, and funders
• Increase funding support for OA publishing
• Prohibit use of funds to support publishing in Predatory journals
• Audit where grantee publish their research
• No promotions, further funding for research
• Declaration to ethics committee
• Condition to work with institute library
• Must check against authentic sources

How the libraries playing their role?
• Start conversation about scholarly publishing process or entire echo system
• Educate researchers- how to select journals for publishing their research
• Create sufficient awareness
• Create resources, tools , support systems, consultations
• Help create policy guidelines
• Be part of the team which assists researchers
• Share information about predatory publishing

• Monitor Institution’s publications
