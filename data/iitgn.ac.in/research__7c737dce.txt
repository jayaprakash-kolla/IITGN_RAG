# Research & Development

 As a hub for cutting-edge research, IITGN is fostering interdisciplinary collaborations to address critical challenges in science, technology, design, and society. Through partnerships with leading global institutions and industries, the institute drives advancements in energy, healthcare, artificial intelligence, sustainability, and materials science. With world-class research facilities, a strong emphasis on translational impact, and a flexible academic structure, IITGN provides an environment that supports innovation, industry collaboration, and international research engagement.

IITGN provides liberal research support to faculty to initiate their research. The seed funding has no specified upper limit, and there is a separate budget for procuring high-end infrastructure.

 IITGN liberally supports travel by faculty and students — both undergraduate and postgraduate — to international conferences. We also support organizing workshops, schools, and conferences at IITGN.

#### Making A Difference

 At IITGN, we emphasize research inspired by both real-world challenges and issues of fundamental importance which also have a longer-term impact on society.

#### Undergraduate Research

 At IITGN, active engagement and participation of undergraduate students in research is greatly encouraged, and various opportunities are built into the the curriculum.

### The Paper Trail to Cancer Detection

 A strip of paper can hold a shopping list, a train ticket, or a child’s drawing. Researchers from IITGN and IIT Jammu are asking it to do something far harder: spot cancer.

 Finding cancer cells can be challenging, especially when abnormal cells are vastly outnumbered by healthy ones. A few even break away from a tumour and drift into the bloodstream, scattered so thinly they are almost impossible to find. The earlier these cells can be identified, the greater the potential for timely intervention. Yet many existing diagnostic approaches can be expensive, time-consuming or dependent on specialised equipment and trained personnel.

### Bringing Clarity Through a Different Lens

 It can be difficult to recognise a face in a blurry, noisy, or damaged photograph. Restoring such images becomes even more challenging when the cause of the damage is unknown or a clean version of the same photograph is unavailable as a reference.

 Addressing this issue, researchers at IIT Gandhinagar and IIT Banaras Hindu University have developed GenR, an AI framework that uses a pretrained face-generating model, StyleGAN3, as a guide for what a realistic human face should look like. Instead of simply sharpening the damaged image, GenR works backward by creating a candidate face, applying an approximation of the damage to it, and then adjusting the generated face until its degraded version resembles the original photograph.

### Consistency in Complexity: New Insights into Quiver Bundles

 𝐂𝐨𝐧𝐬𝐢𝐬𝐭𝐞𝐧𝐜𝐲 𝐢𝐧 𝐂𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐥𝐞𝐱𝐢𝐭𝐲: 𝐍𝐞𝐰 𝐈𝐧𝐬𝐢𝐠𝐡𝐭𝐬 𝐢𝐧𝐭𝐨 𝐐𝐮𝐢𝐯𝐞𝐫 𝐁𝐮𝐧𝐝𝐥𝐞𝐬

 From quantum field theory to string theory, vector bundles and connections provide an important mathematical framework to describe how physical quantities change across space. Researchers at IIT Gandhinagar studied connections, which are rules for describing how vectors change from one point to another, on several vector bundles linked by maps. These structures are called quiver bundles. The researchers studied when connections can be chosen so that they work consistently with all the maps. For tree-shaped quiver bundles, they found that this is possible when the maps maintain the same rank along each path. They also studied what can prevent such connections from existing and how they relate to flat connections and topology. By extending familiar ideas from individual vector bundles to linked systems, this work develops a broader geometric framework that can be useful in theoretical physics.

### When the Monsoon Stops Bringing Relief

 India’s monsoon may no longer bring relief from extreme heat. A new study led by IIT Gandhinagar finds that uncompensable heat stress, which occurs when the body can no longer cool itself well enough, has already increased across India. Today, it is mostly a summer danger. But at 2°C of global warming, it could affect 53% of India during the monsoon, nearly matching the 60% affected by summer. Using realistic human heat limits instead of a simple wet-bulb measure, the study shows how humidity could turn the rainy season into a major health risk. These results highlight that the monsoon is becoming a period of dangerous, unbearable heat for hundreds of millions, providing insights to improve heat alerts, worker safety, and climate policy.

### Caste in Social Media: A Sentiment and Emotion Analysis Study

 A news post appears on Facebook. In the comment section, people react, understand, debate, mourn, rage, and remember. In a study, IITGN researchers analysed 17,601 caste-related posts and over 254,000 comments from Indian newspaper pages between 2012 and 2024. What makes the work stand out is the layered approach they followed. They compared these discussions with an equal number of non-caste ones from the same sources and mapped topics, engagement, sentiment, and 28 emotions together. Caste-related posts drew comments with stronger sentiments and affects such as anger, sadness, disgust, and disapproval, etc., especially around everyday discrimination and local violence. By mapping these emotions, the study offers a way to better understand where caste-based exclusion surfaces online and how digital spaces can also become sites of awareness, resistance, and change.

### Lights, Heat, Action, The Cancer Edition

 What if cancer therapy could locate a cell’s powerhouse and activate only under light? At IIT Gandhinagar, researchers have developed a smart molecule, ‘7a’, that forms tiny nanoparticles, slips into the mitochondria of cancer cells, and emits a glow useful for imaging. When near-infrared light is shone on it, 7a heats up as well as creates reactive oxygen species that harm mitochondria from within. This disruption triggers programmed cell death and autophagy, a stress-response cleanup process inside cancer cells. It also carries an indomethacin drug unit that helps suppress Cox-2, a cancer-linked enzyme. Together, these actions push cancer cells toward death, while causing the least damage to normal cells in the lab. This work may help lead to a future where cancer therapy becomes more precise, less harsh, and guided by light.

### Blink and Miss: A Dilemma of Nanoscale Light Emitters

 Perovskite Nanocrystals (PNCs) hold immense promise for next-gen technology, from ultra-efficient displays to quantum computing. However, a persistent challenge—Photoluminescence blinking—disrupts their performance by causing intermittent light emission. At IITGN’s NanoPlasmonics Research Lab, Dr NVS Praneeth, a Research Associate, is tackling this issue by developing protective coatings for Perovskite and QDs. ### Nature’s QR Code: How DNA Barcoding Deciphers Plant Identity

 Nature is full of lookalikes—plants that appear nearly identical but may belong to entirely different species. This similarity can lead to major challenges for scientists, especially in medicine, agriculture, and conservation. Dr Nilesh Gawande, a postdoctoral fellow at IITGN, is part of a project that is using DNA barcoding for species identification with genetic precision. In a research study with multi-institute contributions, researchers analysed the matK gene and found it to be a robust marker that can differentiate even the most minor variations within closely related plant species.

### Infinite Sums, Precise Outcomes: Exploring New Arithmetic Terrai

 Mathematics can feel like solving a giant puzzle, where numbers hide fascinating secrets. At IITGN, Dr Shashank Ashok Chorge is uncovering some of these secrets using a tool called the Voronoi summation formula. Mathematicians use this formula to improve the “error terms,” which are minor inaccuracies that appear whilst estimating complex problems. The research also assessed how certain sums (called Riesz-type weighted sums) change their "sign" over time—shifting from positive to negative. These shifts are connected to a famous mathematics puzzle about the zeta function's zeros.
