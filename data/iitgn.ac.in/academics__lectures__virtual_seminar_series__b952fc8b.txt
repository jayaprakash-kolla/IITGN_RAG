## What is a black hole?

 View Lecture
 24th April, 2020 | 4 - 5:15 PM

 A black hole is an exotic compact object whose gravitational attraction is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. Black holes were long considered a mathematical curiosity of eccentric theoretical physicists, but recent observational results provided compelling evidence in favour of the existence of these exotic objects in the universe. Astronomers have identified numerous stellar black hole candidates in binary star systems and also established that a source known as Sagittarius A*, at the core of our own Milky Way galaxy, contains a supermassive black hole of about 4.3 million solar masses. In 2016, the LIGO collaboration announced the first direct detection of gravitational waves from two merging black holes. In 2019, the first-ever direct image of a black hole and its vicinity was published, following observations made by the Event Horizon Telescope of the supermassive black hole in the centre of the galaxy Messier 87.

 Black hoes are often considered to be the mathematical laboratory to study the fundamental laws of physics; in the particular relationship between quantum theory and gravitation. A remarkable property of the black hole is that it behaves like a thermodynamic system, with temperature and entropy. It is widely believed that the study of these properties will provide us with clues about the ultimate aim of theoretical physics, the unification of all forces, the theory of everything!

 In this (online) seminar, I will present a non-technical account of the fascinating history of black hole research.

## Dear Virus, Thank you!!!

 View Lecture

 Viruses are again at the centre of human attention. A century ago, they were a big headache for cancer biologists and occupied the centre piece of research in biology. The last two decades shed light on the fact that the world as we know wouldn't exist without the agents of change that these viruses are. For one thing, the apes be would still be apes, not humans, if it wasn't for some viruses. I will share some insights into the love-hate relationship between humans and the beautiful things these viruses are. We will end with a serious note of thanks to the viruses.

## An introduction to the Theory of Partitions

 View Lecture

 The Theory of Partitions is an active branch of number theory and combinatorics. It was initiated by Euler and later, many people such as J. J. Sylvester, S. Ramanujan, G. H. Hardy made important contributions to it. Ramanujan's stunning advances in this field, either by himself or in collaboration with Hardy, opened the doors for many of the current areas within number theory. This talk will be an introduction to this beautiful area of mathematics mainly concentrating on the partition function p(n) and the congruences satisfied by it.

## History and geography of fluid mechanics

 View Lecture
 In this seminar, we take a look at the history of fluid dynamics - we see how the subject was developed from the ancient time and talk about the people who made great contributions towards building the subject. We also take a brief glance at how to quantify fluid motion, what forces and properties play important roles. This helps us get an overview of the subject across scales and to understand what are the different active areas of research in fluid dynamics. We shall try to keep the seminar non-technical and accessible to an audience from a large spectrum of background.
