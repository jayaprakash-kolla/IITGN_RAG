#### Instructor: Pankaj Khanna

 Course Content

### 2022-23 (Semester 2)

# SC 306 : Coral Reefs: Past, Present and Future

 Instructor
 Pankaj Khanna

 About the Course
 In this short course the students will learn about the modern carbonate environments, specifically Coral Reef systems. Students will learn about the past, present, and future of these systems, as well as, the organisms and sediments making these reefs, their composition, minerology, and texture. Students will learn about different depositional environments including forereef, reef flat and lagoon. The course will also focus on the effect of modern-sea-level rise in eroding and inundating islands associated with these settings.

 About the Instructor
 Dr. Pankaj Khanna is a Carbonate Sedimentologist in the Discipline of Earth Sciences at IIT Gandhinagar. Pankaj’s work integrates both field and laboratory investigations, from modern to ancient reefs, and from qualitative to quantitative assessment of carbonate depositional systems. He has conducted fieldwork and training on three continents and in more than ten countries from India to Americas. He has worked on/studied modern reefs in Gulf of Mexico, Belize, Maldives, and currently working on Lakshadweep reefs. He has also participated on a research expedition onboard R/V Falkor, mapping south Texas banks (south Texas Shelf), as well as led a research expedition in Red Sea (Al-Wajh)

 About the Course
 Learning about contemporary practices regarding engineering solutions to tackle freshwater scarcity needs no further emphasis. Government and private agencies working in this field expect young civil, chemical and mechanical engineers to help them in planning, designing and executing of schemes for conveyance, treatment and reuse of sewage.

 In view of the above, it is proposed to conduct a short course, ‘Apna Jal – Reuse of Treated Wastewater. The course is useful for Civil, Chemical and Mechanical stream engineering students aspiring for jobs in water sector.

Guidelines and BIS codes issued recently regarding the reuse of treated wastewater (now called ‘Apna Jal’) will be discussed. In a nutshell - Planning, designing and mandatory issues related to the management of sewage, including its reuse rather than discharge will be taught.

 Instructor
 Cynthia Campoy Brophy (non-IITGN)

 About the Course
 What is conflict, how does it reveal difference, and how can we think of conflict as opportunity? Rather than react to conflict with adversity, we will explore how to engage with our differences, how to deeply listen, and how to recognize and break habits of negative reaction. We will build our capacity to generate creative options to effectively communicate and collaborate as part of our daily interactions with self, family, our communities of friends and work/school colleagues. Conflict at home has similar roots to global conflicts. When we truly listen and understand why conflict arises, we can recognize and acknowledge the underlying emotions, learn to pause, think it through, and then act with respect and curiosity. During this workshop, we will explore how to better understand the underlying needs and interests of conflict, how to develop tools to listen and respond as opposed to react. We will also explore tools of mediation and the value of having a neutral listener help to facilitate party self-determination.

#### (SC 318) Topics and Perspectives in Parallel Computing and Parallel Programming

#### Instructor: Laxmikant Kale

 Course Content

### 2022-23 (Semester 2)

# SC 318 : Topics and Perspectives in Parallel Computing and Parallel Programming

 Instructor
 Laxmikant Kale

 About the Course
 This short course aims to capture the allure and challenges presented by parallel computing, where a large number of computers work together to solve a single computational problem faster. We will begin with a review of the history and the state of the art in parallel computing, and especially the hardware technologies. Current parallel computers, including clusters and supercomputers, consist of a large number of nodes connected by some interconnection network. Each node itself consists of parallel hardware including multiple cores and accelerators. We will briefly review methods for parallel programming within a node, such as OpenMP, to focus more on across-node distributed memory programming. We will discuss scalable parallel algorithms and programming models such as MPI and Charm++. We will then review specific application domains that go beyond traditional applications of high performance parallel computing, including parallel discrete event simulations, and combinatorial search. We conclude with a view of future challenges and opportunities, and how parallel computing will impact you, whether as a practicing engineer or an early-stage researcher. The course assumes basic programming knowledge, using languages such as (one or more of) C, C++, Python, and Fortran. There may be opportunities for writing small parallel programs to increase your familiarity with parallel programming.

#### (SC 319) Introduction to Magnetism & Spintronics

#### Instructor: Jean-Philippe Ansermet (non-IITGN)

 Course Content

### 2022-23 (Semester 2)

# SC 319 : Introduction to Magnetism & Spintronics

 Instructor
 Jean-Philippe Ansermet (non-IITGN)

 About the Course
 The learning outcome of this course is to be able to understand research topics in spintronics, the aim of which is to provide new schemes of doing signal and information processing using the spin of the electron instead of its charge. The basic unit of any spintronics device is magnetic and has dimensions in the nanometer range. In order to understand what is happening in this field, therefore, it is necessary to understand nanomagnetism, spin transport, and spin dynamics.

 The course is organized so as to be accessible at several entry level of competencies in Physics. The lectures entitled “conceptual …” (4 out of 10) are crucial to achieve the learning outcome, yet easily accessible without a background in advanced mathematics, statistical physics, or quantum mechanics. Thermodynamics of irreversible processes will be reviewed and applied to introduce concepts of spin-dependent transport. Simple quantum mechanics calculations will explain fundamental mechanisms such as exchange, magneto-crystalline anisotropies, RKKY and DM couplings. Magnetic resonance and spin relaxation will be introduced with a semi-classical picture.

 About the Instructor
 Prof. Jean-Philippe Ansermet is a Swiss Physicist at École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne [EPFL]. His research focuses on the fabrication and properties of nanostructured materials as well as spintronics. He obtained a diploma as a Physics Engineer at EPFL in 1980. He went on to get a PhD from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, where, from 1985 to 1987, he pursued as a post-doc on solid-state NMR studies of molecules bound to the surface of catalysts. From 1987 to 1992, he worked at the materials research center of Ciba-Geigy, on polymers for microelectronics, composites, dielectrics, and organic charge transfer complexes. In March 1992, as a Professor of Experimental Physics, he developed a laboratory on the theme of nanostructured materials and turned full Professor in 1995. Since 1992, he has taught classical mechanics, first to future engineering students, and since 2004 to Physics majors. Since 2000, he has taught thermodynamics also, to the same group of students. Since 1998, he has offered a spintronics and spin dynamics graduate coursed. His research activities include the fabrication and properties of magnetic nanostructures produced by electrodeposition. His involvement since the early days of spintronics has allowed him to gain recognition for his work on giant magnetoresistance (CPP-GMR), and magnetic relaxation of single nanostructures. He was among the leading groups demonstrating magnetisation reversal by spin-polarised currents. Furthermore, his group uses nuclear magnetic resonance, on the one hand, as means of investigation of surfaces and electrodes, on the other hand, as a local probe of the electronic properties of complex ferromagnetic oxides.

# SC 321 : Seismic risk determination and risk-based assessment of buildings

 Instructor
 Pierino Lestuzzi (non-IITGN)

 About the Course
 This course provides basic knowledge in the topics of seismic risk determination and risk-based assessment of existing buildings. The participants of this course will learn how to compute the seismic risk for buildings, which is a challenging task associated with seismological and structural parameters. They will also learn the background of the risk-based assessment approach used in Switzerland to determine if seismic retrofitting of existing buildings is necessary. The possible translation of such an innovative approach into Indian conditions is not obvious and a critical review of the backgrounds is mandatory before applying such a risk-based assessment in other countries. Comprehensive documentation (research reports, scientific papers and chapter of book) of each topic will be provided to the participants. The course comprises for the last day an application for typical Indian buildings in form of a tutorial.

#### (SC 323) Modern Digital Processors for Industrial and Automotive Applications

#### Instructor: Prof. Mukul Chandorkar, Visiting Professor

 Course Content

### 2022-23 (Semester 2)

# SC 323 : Modern Digital Processors for Industrial and Automotive Applications

 Instructor
 Prof. Mukul Chandorkar, Visiting Professor

 About the Course
 Modern digital processors which are meant for industrial and automotive control applications combine high processing speeds with a rich set of internal peripheral modules and memory. Peripheral modules include analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog converters (ADC and DAC), communication interfaces, timers, sensor interfaces and pulse-width modulated outputs. They have significant amounts of on-chip memory, volatile and non-volatile. Several industrial processors have multiple processing cores.

 This course is meant to expose students to the detailed use of features of digital processors oriented towards industrial and automotive applications. It will also introduce students to the software code-generation tool-chains of these processors. The course will use the Texas Instruments TMS320F28379D dual-core digital processor as an example to illustrate features useful for industrial and automotive applications.

 About the Instructor
 Prof. Mukul Chandorkar has been a faculty member in the Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Bombay, since 2000. He has B. Tech., M. Tech. and Ph.D. degrees from IIT Bombay, IIT Madras and the University of Wisconsin- Madison, respectively. He specializes in power electronics and electric machines. Before joining IIT Bombay, he worked with power electronics companies in India, the USA and Europe. He is currently on sabbatical leave from IIT Bombay and is a visiting professor at IIT Gandhinagar.
